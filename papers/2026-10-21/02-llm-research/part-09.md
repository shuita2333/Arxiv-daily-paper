### 401. [Procedural Memory for Language Agents: A Neurosymbolic Perspective on Explicit Goal Structure](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.25996)

**<font color=#1a73e8>作者：</font>** Jonathan Leung, Zhiqi Shen, Deheng Ye  
**<font color=#188038>arXiv所属领域：</font>** Artificial Intelligence

**<font color=#5f6368>摘要：</font>**
> Large language models remain unreliable on long-horizon tasks requiring procedural reasoning. We argue that a key cause is the absence of explicit procedural memory: accessible, modifiable knowledge of how tasks are accomplished, distinct from semantic and episodic memory. Goal-Oriented Graphs offer such a representation, encoding goals and prerequisite dependencies as inspectable symbolic structure. We examine their construction, the challenges they pose, and principles for integrating them with neural reasoning.

### 402. [TempoMoE: Identifying and Steering Fast-Slow Thinking Experts for Efficient Multimodal Reasoning](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.25999)

**<font color=#1a73e8>作者：</font>** Xiyue Zhang, Keru Zhou, Ziyi Wang 等 5 位作者  
**<font color=#188038>arXiv所属领域：</font>** Computation and Language

**<font color=#5f6368>摘要：</font>**
> Multimodal Mixture-of-Experts (MoE) reasoning models rely on long chain-of-thought (CoT) for complex tasks, yet often overthink easy inputs, increasing token cost and latency, and sometimes degrading accuracy. Existing methods optimize reasoning length during training or impose external control at inference, but leave the underlying reasoning mechanism a black box. We find that fast and slow thinking exhibit distinct expert-routing patterns: holding the input fixed and varying only the prompted reasoning tempo produces stable routing shifts. Suppressing slow-thinking experts shortens the reasoning chain, whereas suppressing fast-thinking experts lengthens it, providing causal evidence that these experts influence reasoning depth. Based on this finding, we propose TempoMoE, a training-free, two-stage framework. It identifies fast- and slow-thinking experts via tempo-contrastive routing analysis and redistributes routing weights within the original top-k set according to sample difficulty. It amplifies fast-thinking experts and suppresses slow-thinking experts on easy inputs and reverses this adjustment on hard ones, enabling difficulty-adaptive control over reasoning depth. We evaluate TempoMoE on Kimi-VL, Qwen3-VL, and ERNIE-4.5-VL across six benchmarks. Compared with Vanilla, TempoMoE consistently improves accuracy while reducing output tokens, with accuracy gains of up to 2.6 points and token reductions of up to 35.4%.

### 403. [SPLASH: Contrastive Audit Pressure for Detecting and Localizing Security Hallucinations in Generated Code](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.26001)

**<font color=#1a73e8>作者：</font>** Mirza Masfiqur Rahman, MD Ajwad Akil, Imtiaz Karim 等 6 位作者  
**<font color=#188038>arXiv所属领域：</font>** Cryptography and Security

**<font color=#5f6368>摘要：</font>**
> Code generated by large language models often passes every functional test while remaining insecure. These security hallucinations are common, written with high confidence, and repeated across samples, so neither the generator's own uncertainty nor static analyzers reliably catch them. We introduce SPLASH, which is guided by a new measure called audit pressure. It contrasts teacher-forced log-probabilities for the generated program under a security review and a style review. Aggregated to statements and programs, and grounded in a probabilistic model of competing edits, audit pressure allows SPLASH to choose the secure program among a model's candidate programs and locate the vulnerable statement, with no model training, CWE-specific rules, or internal access to the generating model. On 63,684 programs generated by 12 models for 899 tasks, SPLASH picks secure code in 74% of cases, places the vulnerable statement first in 58% of insecure programs, and chooses better than a frontier LLM judge.

### 404. [TimesJev: Thinking Fast on Time Series with System 1 Sufficiency](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.26002)

**<font color=#1a73e8>作者：</font>** Chenghao Liu, Wei Fan, Jindong Han 等 7 位作者  
**<font color=#188038>arXiv所属领域：</font>** Artificial Intelligence

**<font color=#5f6368>摘要：</font>**
> Large language models (LLMs) have recently shown strong potential for time series reasoning, while models that reason over time series are usually with long chains of thought. However, this process often consumes thousands of tokens, directly leading to substantial latency during inference. In contrast, System 1 models such as Jev make decisions instantly and can naturally be an alternative, but raw time series are not compatible with the embedding space of these models, thus hindering the reasoning performance, especially when input series contain thousands of numeric points. In this paper, we make a very early attempt to propose a time series System 1 model, namely TimesJev. Specifically, we first formalize System 1 sufficiency in time series reasoning, which motivates a compressed representation for time series with key frequency patterns preserved. To construct this representation, we derive a dimension reduction objective that combines (i) a sufficiency divergence that measures the core information lost during compression, and (ii) a realizability divergence that quantifies the incompatibility of the System 1 model to the optimal prediction given this representation. We optimize this objective by learning time series compression with a gating mechanism for sufficiency, and then aligning the selected representation with the embedding space for realizability. Finally, experiments on 13 datasets demonstrate that TimesJev outperforms reasoning models by over 62% with 62.9% less inference time than their fine-tuned versions on average.

### 405. [Scaling Synthetic Pre-training Data and Evaluation for Indic LLMs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.26006)

**<font color=#1a73e8>作者：</font>** Abhinav Khattar, Aishwarya Chhabra, Ameya Velingker 等 27 位作者  
**<font color=#188038>arXiv所属领域：</font>** Computation and Language

**<font color=#5f6368>摘要：</font>**
> Building capable language models for India requires scalable multilingual data and robust evaluation. We introduce Nemotron-Pretraining-Indic-v1, a synthetic pretraining corpus of approximately 2.83 trillion tokens covering all 22 scheduled Indian languages in native script and twelve in romanized form. It is the largest openly released Indic pretraining corpus. Using our GPU-accelerated pipeline, we translate curated English documents, assess translation quality, and repair low-quality outputs while preserving technical content and document structure and maintaining source traceability. Through continued-pretraining experiments across twelve languages, we show that combining our synthetic corpus with natural Indic text improves knowledge and reasoning over a public-corpus baseline. Additional experiments show how data volume, script coverage, and Indic training share affect English performance. We also introduce the Indic-LLM-Eval-Suite, a multilingual benchmark suite for evaluating Indic language models across chat, instruction following, reasoning, knowledge, code, and translation. The suite combines existing Indic benchmarks with task-aware translations of English benchmarks, using automated quality assessment, targeted correction, and native-speaker review in a human-in-the-loop pipeline. We use the suite to comprehensively benchmark contemporary open-weight Indic and multilingual models across capabilities and languages, revealing substantial variation across tasks and languages. Together, these resources provide a scalable foundation for developing and evaluating more capable Indic language models.

### 406. [Accelerated Drift of Scientific Prose in the LLM Era: Evidence from an Interrupted Time-Series Analysis](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.26008)

**<font color=#1a73e8>作者：</font>** Hanna Hubarava, Benjamin V. Ineichen  
**<font color=#188038>arXiv所属领域：</font>** Computation and Language

**<font color=#5f6368>摘要：</font>**
> Every scientific text is embedded in the writing norms of its time: it reflects earlier publications while also shaping the texts that follow. The growing use of LLM-based writing tools introduces a new factor in this evolving system, but the linguistic changes coinciding with their emergence remain insufficiently understood. We investigate whether the trajectories of scientific-preprint writing changed in rate or direction after conversational LLMs became publicly accessible, using the release of ChatGPT in November 2022 as the observational breakpoint. We compare pre- and post-2022 trajectories across vocabulary, punctuation, readability, and syntax in abstracts from arXiv's artificial intelligence, statistics, and quantitative biology categories and from medRxiv. Using interrupted time-series regression, we find substantial post-2022 changes across multiple feature families and research domains, including increases in average word length, reduced formula-based readability, and declines in maximum dependency-tree depth and sentence-level depth variation. Individual features follow different dynamics: some lexical markers accelerate persistently, while others rise and then recede. Our findings show that isolated marker frequencies capture only part of the shift: changes in the rate, direction, and structure of linguistic trajectories provide complementary evidence of broader post-2022 change in scientific-preprint writing. More broadly, these findings motivate monitoring scientific writing as a multidimensional, evolving system rather than through a fixed set of LLM-associated markers.

### 407. [Understanding When Reflective Prompt and Skill Optimization Helps on Locally Served Reasoning Models: A Two-Model Study of GEPA and SkillOpt](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.26020)

**<font color=#1a73e8>作者：</font>** Andrea Giummarra, Carmelo Baglieri, Antonio Di Maria 等 6 位作者  
**<font color=#188038>arXiv所属领域：</font>** Artificial Intelligence

**<font color=#5f6368>摘要：</font>**
> Reflective text-space optimizers such as GEPA and SkillOpt have reported large gains on frontier and mid-size models. We test replication across two reasoning-native target-serving configurations: DeepSeek V4 Flash through DwarfStar and Qwen3.8-27B through LM Studio. For each target, we evaluate the same held-out tasks and four conditions - minimal baseline, GEPA prompt, SkillOpt skill, and manually engineered prompt - on HotpotQA (n=500 per condition) and MATH-500 (n=250). In the primary Qwen v2 rerun under the same frozen protocol, GEPA selects the HotpotQA seed and exactly reproduces baseline behavior, whereas SkillOpt's validation gain reverses into held-out degradation. On MATH-500, GEPA raises exact match from 0.828 to 0.904 while reducing latency but increasing token consumption, so lower response time does not imply lower token cost. DeepSeek likewise shows its strongest GEPA effect on MATH-500 (0.852 to 0.908). Across both targets, GEPA's strongest gains occur on MATH-500, while neither optimizer improves the HotpotQA baseline. Optimization is therefore task- and method-dependent, and validation gains may reverse on held-out evaluation. An earlier Qwen run is retained as serving-sensitivity evidence. These results suggest that textual headroom for a model-task pair and the observed effect of a text-space optimizer are more informative when decomposed into content/reasoning, answer-realization/evaluator alignment, and serving-completion/reliability. The cross-benchmark pattern further suggests that optimization may benefit from low-ambiguity, actionable evaluation signals.

### 408. [FrameGRPO: Incentivizing Video Reasoning via Multi-Frame Rollouts and Temporal Credit](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.26022)

**<font color=#1a73e8>作者：</font>** Jiaxuan Sun, Biao Wang, Shuai Li  
**<font color=#188038>arXiv所属领域：</font>** Computer Vision and Pattern Recognition

**<font color=#5f6368>摘要：</font>**
> Reinforcement learning with verifiable rewards, particularly Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO), has improved video reasoning in multimodal large language models. However, standard video GRPO varies only linguistic trajectories while conditioning every rollout in a group on the same sampled frames, which can produce homogeneous rewards and weaken group-relative learning when the shared visual evidence is insufficient or distracting. We propose \textbf{FrameGRPO}, a simple and effective reinforcement-learning framework that requires neither auxiliary networks nor changes to the model architecture. Specifically, FrameGRPO combines two lightweight components: (1) \textbf{Multi-Frame Rollouts}, which use only uniform temporal subsampling of a single maximal-frame decode to construct aligned views that diversify visual evidence and reasoning trajectories; and (2) \textbf{Gated Temporal Efficiency Credit}, which selectively reinforces a correct lower-ratio trajectory when all rollouts in a fixed full-ratio reference subset fail, without imposing an unconditional preference for fewer frames. Extensive experiments across diverse video understanding and reasoning benchmarks demonstrate robust overall improvements over standard GRPO, highlighting temporal-view diversification as an effective mechanism for exploration and credit assignment in video MLLMs.

### 411. [FinHopper: A Progressive Multi-Hop Benchmark for Complex Financial Agents with Step-wise Evaluation](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.26036)

**<font color=#5f6368>摘要：</font>**
> Financial agents are expected to answer factual investment queries through open search, but financial multi-hop retrieval differs from general multi-hop QA in a crucial way: each intermediate answer is an exact typed constraint, such as an entity, date, event, or numerical indicator, that determines the next query. This strict answer-conditioned dependency makes small intermediate errors cascade into plausible but wrong search trajectories. We introduce FinHopper, a benchmark for complex financial agents with controlled reasoning depth from 1 to 6 hops, covering stocks, funds, and macroeconomic facts, with gold intermediate answers for process-level evaluation. Experiments with 20 LLM-agent configurations show that public search tools are necessary but insufficient: performance drops sharply as hop depth increases, the best model remains far below human experts, and process-aware scoring reveals partial reasoning progress hidden by final-answer-only evaluation. ### 412. [Balance-Aware Pivot Selection for Parallel Diffusion LLM Decoding](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.26040)

**<font color=#5f6368>摘要：</font>**
> Diffusion language models enable parallel token generation, offering a promising alternative to sequential autoregressive decoding. Existing acceleration methods use predictive confidence to enable parallel token commitment while preserving generation quality. However, confidence-based selection prioritizes reliable token predictions without explicitly considering how token positions shape the remaining decoding schedule. Moreover, such ordering does not always reflect inter-token dependencies, suggesting that confidence need not be the sole criterion for token selection. Our practical method accounts for model uncertainty by combining confidence-based prefilling with confidence-thresholded center selection. We evaluate the resulting quality-efficiency tradeoff on LLaDA and Dream across five mathematical reasoning, code generation, and instruction-following benchmarks. ### 414. [Procedural Exposure Bias in Skill-Augmented Language Model Agents](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.26062)

**<font color=#1a73e8>作者：</font>** Qing Yang, Yuqi Xiong, Pengcheng Huang 等 8 位作者  
**<font color=#188038>arXiv所属领域：</font>** Computer Vision and Pattern Recognition

**<font color=#5f6368>摘要：</font>**
> External skills provide language-model agents with reusable procedural knowledge, yet how models use such knowledge after it is supplied remains poorly understood. We identify Procedural Exposure Bias (PEB), the tendency of language models to increasingly favor an action as its associated procedural pattern is repeatedly exposed in a skill, even when the interaction state and action relevance remain unchanged. We find that PEB strongly depends on how procedural knowledge is represented: presenting the same content visually substantially attenuates exposure-induced shifts in action preference relative to textual presentation. Further analysis shows that this attenuation primarily arises from visual encoding rather than structured layout. Although repeated procedural signals remain recoverable from Visual representations, they exert a weaker influence on downstream action prediction, distinguishing signal accessibility from its use as action evidence. This effect also extends to executable action selection, where increasing exposure more strongly favors the corresponding action under Text than Visual. Layer-wise analyses show that exposure-dependent action evidence becomes more pronounced toward the model output, with stronger evidence under Text, while activation interventions reveal architecture-specific roles of internal components in transmitting or counteracting the preference shift. Complete-agent evaluations across ALFWorld, ScienceWorld, and WebShop further show that Visual skills achieve higher macro-average task success than Text skills for all four evaluated models. These findings establish skill representation as an important factor governing how procedural knowledge is translated into agent decisions. All code is available at this https URL.

### 417. [DCR-TSF: Dual-Level Compositional Reasoning with Triadic Spherical Fusion for Training-Free Zero-Shot Composed Image Retrieval](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.26078)

**<font color=#1a73e8>作者：</font>** Gwang-Ho Na, Ho-Joong Kim, Seong-Whan Lee  
**<font color=#188038>arXiv所属领域：</font>** Computer Vision and Pattern Recognition

**<font color=#5f6368>摘要：</font>**
> Zero-shot composed image retrieval (ZS-CIR) aims to retrieve a target image that reflects an intended textual modification while maintaining semantic consistency with a reference image. Existing training-free ZS-CIR methods typically rely on fine-grained generated target descriptions, which often overemphasize reference-specific visual details and lead to appearance-biased retrieval. Moreover, since these descriptions are generated through multimodal large language models (MLLMs), the direct influence of the original image-text query can be weakened in the final retrieval representation. In this paper, we propose DCR-TSF, a training-free ZS-CIR framework that combines Dual-level Compositional Reasoning (DCR) and Triadic Spherical Fusion (TSF). DCR generates fine- and coarse-grained descriptions from the reference image and modification text, capturing complementary target-side semantics across different granularities. To preserve the original image-text query information, TSF constructs a composed-query anchor by spherically interpolating the reference image feature and modification text feature, and fuses it with the fine- and coarse-grained representations using a weighted Karcher mean on the unit hypersphere. The resulting final query representation balances detailed visual evidence, high-level semantic abstraction, and direct preservation of the original composed query. Extensive experiments on CIRCO, CIRR, and Fashion-IQ demonstrate that our method achieves state-of-the-art performance among training-free methods.

### 418. [LeakBench: Auditing Reward Hacking through Prediction-Time Leakage in Clinical Modeling Agents](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.26088)

**<font color=#1a73e8>作者：</font>** Xiaoyuan Bao, Jingsi Jiang, Jiao Shan 等 7 位作者

**<font color=#5f6368>摘要：</font>**
> Autonomous clinical-modeling agents can optimize predictive performance while violating conditions that make a model usable. LeakBench combines a synthetic task with known failure structure and two real-data settings: MIMIC-IV (430k admissions) and governed multi-hospital inpatient record front-page (MHFP) data. ### 419. [From Code to Playable Systems: CEGame-Bench for Long-Horizon Game Creation Agents](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.26098)

**<font color=#1a73e8>作者：</font>** Zixuan Dong, Yanghai Wang, Hanyan Bian 等 11 位作者  
**<font color=#188038>arXiv所属领域：</font>** Artificial Intelligence

**<font color=#5f6368>摘要：</font>**
> A game ships when a player can launch it, finish it, and return to a saved world, none of which a successful build guarantees, yet game-creation agents are mostly tested on one engine or on one-prompt browser games.
