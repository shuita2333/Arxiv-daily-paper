### 2. [Faculty Experience with Generative AI Integration into the Teaching and Learning Process](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.27998)

**<font color=#1a73e8>作者：</font>** Brent A. Anders, Anait Akopyan  
**<font color=#188038>arXiv所属领域：</font>** Human-Computer Interaction

**<font color=#5f6368>摘要：</font>**
> This study was conducted to enhance our knowledge and understanding of the lived experiences of faculty regarding generative artificial intelligence (AI) in teaching and learning within the English and Communication Program at the American University of Armenia. This phenomenological qualitative research study used face-to-face, in-depth, open-ended interviews with five faculty members from the program. Faculty expressed different levels of conflict, voicing both positive and negative issues and concerns regarding their experiences with generative AI in the teaching and learning process. Five categories and 11 themes emerged from the analysis of these interviews, including concerns about academic integrity and student learning, as well as a lack of faculty AI training, understanding, and experience. Emergent themes indicated that faculty need additional support, including training and leadership guidance, in order to better understand the importance and benefits of effective AI utilization and AI literacy development. Faculty lacked sufficient experience with and understanding of generative AI capabilities and ongoing developments. Overall, faculty perceptions and attitudes toward generative AI within teaching and learning can be characterized as a mix of reservation and optimism.

### 3. [Construct Validity of AI-Administered Cognitive Assessment: Psychometric Validation of a Nine-Dimension Process-Oriented Evaluation Instrument](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.28000)

**<font color=#1a73e8>作者：</font>** Abdalla Doleh, Toni Somers, Ratna Babu Chinnam  
**<font color=#188038>arXiv所属领域：</font>** Human-Computer Interaction

**<font color=#5f6368>摘要：</font>**
> Process-oriented evaluation of large language models requires psychometrically defensible instruments, yet the measurement tools used in AI cognitive assessment have received little formal validation. This paper reports the construct validation of a 54-item, nine-dimension instrument operationalizing the Multi-Dimensional Assessment for AI Cognition framework through a repeated six-component AI-administered adjudication architecture. The validation program combined conceptual audit, schema-integrity testing, confirmatory factor analysis, and targeted revision across multiple versions, each validated on a fresh cohort of roughly 1,800 to 2,200 scenario-response pairs.
The best current version (v4.7, n = 1,774) achieves acceptable to good psychometric properties across all nine dimensions. All meet internal consistency thresholds, with Cronbach's alpha from 0.770 to 0.922. Confirmatory factor analysis shows four dimensions at good fit and four at acceptable fit, with 53 of 54 items loading at or above 0.40 on their parent factor. Schema enforcement yields perfect structural integrity, confirming the six-component architecture as stable. One item, Hallucination Control Q6, remains below threshold but improved materially under v4.7's evidential calibration redesign, surpassing prior versions without reaching target. The instrument is publishable as a largely validated assessment with one sub-threshold component and a deferred tools-enabled comparison reserved for future work. The program also yields a five-category taxonomy of item-level failure modes, including ceiling and floor compression, construct drift, proxy-formula failure, condition confounding, and fundamental mismatch, that may generalize to other AI evaluation contexts.

### 4. [Social-Qwen: From Individual Nonverbal Cues and Emotion to Multiparty Social Dynamics Understanding with Instruction Tuning](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.28001)

**<font color=#1a73e8>作者：</font>** Tung Nguyen, Jouh Yeong Chew  
**<font color=#188038>arXiv所属领域：</font>** Human-Computer Interaction

**<font color=#5f6368>摘要：</font>**
> Effective participation in multiparty scenarios requires robots to move beyond individual toward understanding group-level social dynamics, which are inherently complex due to the interplay of nonverbal cues, internal states, and interaction context. Existing approaches often rely on end-to-end deterministic models, while recent state-of-the-art methods such as large Vision-Language Models (VLMs) address this issue to some extent but remain limited by their size and computational cost for real-time applications. Moreover, both approaches are constrained by the scarcity of multiparty interaction data and annotations, which describe how individual nonverbal cues and emotional states contribute to social dynamics which describe collective outcomes such as group engagement. We hypothesize that explicitly modeling individual-level states is essential for accurate group-level understanding. To this end, we present Social-Qwen, a two-stage framework that first analyzes each participant's nonverbal cues and emotions, then infers group-level engagement using instruction-tuned representations. To mitigate the lack of individual annotations in group datasets, we employ knowledge distillation to transfer supervision signals. Experiments on the OUC-CGE dataset show that Social-Qwen significantly outperforms prior end-to-end baselines and achieves state-of-the-art performance in group engagement analysis, demonstrating the promise of instruction tuning for scalable social intelligence in robots. We further evaluate robustness by testing generalization to (1) an in-house dataset spanning multiple social activities and (2) estimating other social dynamics such as group harmony. Results suggest consistent performance, highlighting Social-Qwen as a promising approach toward real-time social intelligence for intelligent agents.

### 5. [Scrub Data: A Framework for Reproducible Data Curation with AI Coding Agents](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.28003)

**<font color=#1a73e8>作者：</font>** Justin Kay, Shir Bar, Sara Beery  
**<font color=#188038>arXiv所属领域：</font>** Human-Computer Interaction

**<font color=#5f6368>摘要：</font>**
> Data curation---the process of collecting, cleaning, and joining raw datasets for unified analysis---is a time-consuming yet crucial part of any data science project. With the emergence of powerful agentic coding tools, it is tempting to "vibe curate"---i.e., to prompt AI agents to perform data curation operations and blindly trust their outputs in order to finish work more quickly. However, this can exacerbate two key challenges that already plague manual data curation workflows: reproducibility---the ability to trace the exact set of modifications applied to raw datasets---and verifiability---the ability to audit changes and confirm that data is processed properly. To address these issues, we introduce Scrub Data, a framework that enables the use of AI agents in data curation workflows while transparently maintaining both reproducibility and verifiability. The framework involves an interactive data curation loop between a user and an AI agent, backed by a data provenance graph that tracks and versions all dataset updates. The data graph requires that all updates are formatted as self-contained executable steps, allowing any version of a dataset to be reconstructed from raw data by playing forward the transformations stored in the graph. This workflow takes place within a lightweight web application that is designed to be modified by users and agents in real-time to create custom visualization tools for identifying data curation needs and verifying outcomes. We demonstrate the usefulness of the framework with a real data curation use case from animal movement ecology. Starting with 89M raw GPS coordinates, we use the framework to curate a benchmark dataset of 2.5M coordinates to be used for machine learning or statistical analysis. The framework is available for use and extension at this https URL.

### 7. [Social sycophancy differs across access modes of large language models](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.28012)

**<font color=#1a73e8>作者：</font>** Alex Klem, Uwe Peters  
**<font color=#188038>arXiv所属领域：</font>** Human-Computer Interaction

**<font color=#5f6368>摘要：</font>**
> Large language models (LLMs) can display sycophancy, a tendency to excessively praise, agree with, or validate a user. Existing studies have tested LLMs for sycophancy primarily through application programming interfaces (APIs) rather than the consumer user interfaces (UIs) through which the public commonly interacts with LLMs. Access mode (API vs. UI) may affect observed sycophancy rates. However, it remains unknown to what extent prominent models show API-UI differences in social sycophancy, which includes behaviors potentially more likely in UIs (e.g., softening warranted criticism). We tested ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini, and DeepSeek through both APIs and UIs on four dimensions of social sycophancy. Across models, UI access was generally associated with greater social sycophancy, with odds ratios up to 7.56 (95% CI [6.22, 9.20]). However, UI-API differences varied significantly across models for all sycophancy dimensions, including a pronounced reversal for DeepSeek moral sycophancy (43% UI vs. 82.3% API). Overall, our results suggest that API-based LLM evaluations may underestimate the prevalence of sycophancy in corresponding consumer-facing systems, making access conditions consequential for the external validity of LLM evaluation. To facilitate research on access-mode effects, we release 57,000 model responses and SycoUI, a browser-automation pipeline for standardized cross-interface LLM evaluation.

### 9. [Misalignment Between Educators' Testing and End Users' Behavior: What 17,000+ Chatbot Messages Reveal About AI Literacy Training for Teachers](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.28015)

**<font color=#1a73e8>作者：</font>** Ruiwei Xiao, Jiayi Wang, Miina Koyama 等 8 位作者  
**<font color=#188038>arXiv所属领域：</font>** Human-Computer Interaction

**<font color=#5f6368>摘要：</font>**
> AI literacy training for educators is expanding rapidly, but its effectiveness rests largely on self-report, knowledge tests, or interviews, revealing little about whether learning carries into practice or affects students and colleagues who use what educators build. We implemented AI for Learning, an asynchronous online course that trained 87 higher-education educators to recognize AI's capabilities, limitations, and ethics; apply prompt engineering; and evaluate AI outputs. ### 10. [Do Simulated Users Push Back Like Real Users? Correctness vs. Stance](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.28024)

**<font color=#1a73e8>作者：</font>** Xindi Huang  
**<font color=#188038>arXiv所属领域：</font>** Human-Computer Interaction

**<font color=#5f6368>摘要：</font>**
> LLM-simulated users stand in for people in multi-turn evaluations, on the assumption that they react to what the assistant says. We test this for pushback. From WildChat, we take conversations in which the assistant explicitly challenges or endorses a position the user stated, and compare the real next user turn with simulated turns from the same prefix. Among users who replied, real users push back more after a challenge than after an endorsement (20.5% vs. 8.2%); a GPT-based simulator does not (27.1% vs. 27.1%): stance gap D = -0.12 [-0.17, -0.08]. After a challenge the simulator instead often re-states or re-interprets its request ("I meant ..."); about half of these turns are disguised insistence, which puts D between -0.08 and -0.05. What drives the simulator's disputing is whether the assistant's position is judged correct (21% vs. 49% after challenges; 16% vs. 58% after endorsements), while real users change little (19% vs. 24%; 7% vs. 12%), under every outcome definition we tried. Real same-stance examples do not fix this, and another GPT-based simulator and two Claude simulators show stance effects close to real users. Stance sensitivity should be measured for each simulator, not assumed.

### 11. [Measuring Human-AI and AI-AI Teams in Multi-turn Layout Creation](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.28025)

**<font color=#1a73e8>作者：</font>** Rajvir Singh Naginder Singh Virdi, Yewen Pu  
**<font color=#188038>arXiv所属领域：</font>** Human-Computer Interaction

**<font color=#5f6368>摘要：</font>**
> Co-creating layouts such as websites and slides is a useful testbed for evaluating multi-turn instruction following of LLM systems. Since an intended layout's appearance cannot be well expressed in a single turn, the user must wait for the LLM to generate a layout then iteratively refine it with the LLM over multiple turns. We present a small-scale, exploratory (n=6) study of this multi-turn process to compare the differences of human-AI and AI-AI teams on the same layout-generation task. Given a reference wire-frame layout, a participant or a language model 'speaker' issues natural-language instructions to a fixed 'listener' LLM over multiple turns until the speaker is satisfied. Rather than an LLM as the judge, we fit a scoring function over geometric layout features using curated human preference data (n=19). We find that LLMs tend to encode the entire layout in the first turn, while humans iterate over many turns on average, writing much shorter instructions. We also find that human-AI teams consistently scored lower than AI-AI teams. This gap is driven by a limiter of human participants in specifying precise sizes and positions (e.g. 42 pixels to the right). Humans perform almost at par with language models on other features like ordering the elements into nested structures. We then perform interventions on the speaker LLMs to make them more human-like, and find it difficult. This raises the challenge of using LLMs as user models to evaluate multi-turn co-creation tasks.

### 12. [Choosing a Side is not Enough: Epistemic Transparency Under Knowledge Conflict](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.28032)

**<font color=#1a73e8>作者：</font>** Xiaoliang Luo, Emma Rath, Dom Dathan 等 4 位作者  
**<font color=#188038>arXiv所属领域：</font>** Human-Computer Interaction

**<font color=#5f6368>摘要：</font>**
> LLMs are increasingly used in professional knowledge work, where their outputs can influence decisions with real-world consequences. When a model encounters information that contradicts its prior knowledge, it should communicate this conflict transparently to the user. Existing work on knowledge conflicts focuses on which source, parametric or contextual, a model chooses, rather than whether models disclose the presence of the conflict. Moreover, existing work primarily studies multiple-choice questions under simplified counterfactual evidence generation schemes. In this work, we address the first gap by scoring model responses against a taxonomy of epistemic transparency, rather than measuring accuracy alone. We address the second gap by proposing a general framework for generating knowledge conflicts grounded in advanced domain knowledge, which we instantiate in this work as a set of real-world tasks based on counterfactual evidence. These contributions allow us to measure epistemic transparency under realistic knowledge conflicts, as well as the impact of the format and plausibility of the evidence, and the real-world stakes of the task. We find substantial differences in transparency across five evaluated frontier models, with none fully matching our definition of optimal, risk-sensitive epistemic alignment. We hope that our work will ground future discussion of epistemic transparency in real-world applications of LLMs, and help establish a risk framework for epistemic transparency across domains and deployment scenarios.

### 13. [Distributionally-Verifiable Rewards for Human-Behavior Simulation](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.28048)

**<font color=#1a73e8>作者：</font>** Ruoyun Ma, Shuang Liu, Peiye Liu 等 5 位作者  
**<font color=#188038>arXiv所属领域：</font>** Computation and Language

**<font color=#5f6368>摘要：</font>**
> Large language models are increasingly used to simulate survey respondents in social-science research. A useful simulator should predict individual responses, reproduce population response distributions, and capture demographic differences. Common training approaches struggle to balance these goals: exact-match rewards encourage the model to concentrate on the most frequent human response, which is contradictory to the goal of distributional alignment. Meanwhile, aggregate distribution matching can look good even when demographic information is ignored. To this end, we introduce Distributionally-Verifiable Rewards (DVR), a reinforcement-learning reward that uses demographic group distribution gaps to guide individual responses, designed by aligning policy generated frequencies with human response frequencies within each question-group margins. We evaluate the method using the OpinionQA dataset and find that comparing with accuracy-only RLVR, DVR preserves per-response accuracy while reducing JSD by 34.5% in-distribution and 14.6% on held-out questions, with higher DPC point estimates on both splits. Comparing KL-SFT with the demographics-blind marginal further shows that low JSD is insufficient to establish fidelity to population heterogeneity, highlighting that accuracy, distributional fit, and demographic structure should be evaluated together.

### 14. [Benchmarking and Adapting LLMs for English-Swedish Medical Question Answering](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.28050)

**<font color=#1a73e8>作者：</font>** Seyed Alireza Molavi, Zhan Su, Stefan Byttner 等 6 位作者  
**<font color=#188038>arXiv所属领域：</font>** Computation and Language

**<font color=#5f6368>摘要：</font>**
> Large language models do well on English medical exams, but less is known about Swedish. This matters most for small open models that hospitals can run locally. To reduce this gap, we first build an item-aligned English-Swedish benchmark from ten medical multiple-choice datasets; each Swedish item keeps the options and answer of its English source. A conservative refinement loop repairs the translations: open models from three families find errors, ground them in a Swedish medical term base, and keep a model-written edit only if blind judges vote for it. On this benchmark, small models lose much more accuracy in Swedish than large ones, especially when they answer without reasoning. Second, we ask whether English data and open teacher models can close this gap. We translate the training data, let teachers write reasoning traces, and fine-tune two small students on gold answers or traces in English, Swedish, or both. Training on reasoning traces that include Swedish closes the gap and lifts the 1.2B student from 23.5\% to 52.0\% in Swedish, slightly above a 20B open model in its fast mode, with a sixth of its memory. We release the benchmark, its edit history, and the code.

### 15. [Adversarial Attacks Expose Path-Dependent Failures in Discrete Medical Image Tokenizers](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.28078)

**<font color=#1a73e8>作者：</font>** Ahmed Loughzali, Akash Yallamati, Aadiv Reki 等 5 位作者  
**<font color=#188038>arXiv所属领域：</font>** Computer Vision and Pattern Recognition

**<font color=#5f6368>摘要：</font>**
> Discrete image tokenizers convert medical images into token sequences that downstream models either decode into an image for a classifier (the reconstruction path), classify directly (the token path), or feed to a generative multimodal model. We ask whether imperceptible input perturbations can break these systems through the tokenizer alone. We evaluate four tokenizers on three imaging modalities and extend the strongest attack to a deployed generative model. Our central finding is path-dependence: the same tokenizer fails in opposite ways depending on how its output is used. A supervised attack collapses diagnostic accuracy on the reconstruction path, with ROC AUC falling to $\leq 0.013$ at $\epsilon=2/255$ on all four tokenizers, while sparing the token path. An unsupervised, label-free attack does the reverse; its damage concentrates in the multi-codebook tokenizers, worst in MedITok, whose token-path AUC falls from 0.919 to 0.239. The same label-free attack transfers to LLaVA-Med, a frozen medical vision-language model built on MedITok, making it report proliferative diabetic retinopathy in a healthy retina or call a malignant lesion benign, without access to the question, answer, or language-model gradients. Harm is driven by how far the attack moves the tokenizer's internal representation, not by how many tokens change: on the learned-codebook tokenizers, a control attack that changes nearly all tokens but moves the representation less leaves every path and answer intact. Domain-specific pretraining does not provide robustness: MedITok has the best clean accuracy yet is the most fragile, more so than UniTok, its general-domain counterpart. None of six candidate defenses repairs every failure mode, and several fail once the attacker optimizes against the defense directly. Medical tokenizer robustness must therefore be evaluated per downstream path, against an adaptive adversary.

### 16. [Vision-Language Truck Driving Advisories Grounded in Provincial Rules and Forecast Risks](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.28079)

**<font color=#1a73e8>作者：</font>** Xintong Jiang, Shucheng Huang, Qiaoxuan Zhang 等 5 位作者  
**<font color=#188038>arXiv所属领域：</font>** Computer Vision and Pattern Recognition

**<font color=#5f6368>摘要：</font>**
> Vision-language models (VLMs) can generate context-aware driving advisories, but their outputs may conflict with road conditions or traffic rules, and they typically address a hazard only once it is visible. This matters for long-haul trucking, where heavy trucks need long stopping distances and a single trip may cross several jurisdictions with different traffic regulations. This paper proposes a data generation and distillation framework that integrates structured scene understanding, risk forecasting, and traffic-rule retrieval to generate truck driving decisions and natural-language advisories, with a consistency check on every record. The records, written by an 8B teacher, are then used to distill a 3B-parameter student model for onboard deployment. We also introduce a dashcam dataset covering four Canadian provinces, comprising 11,142 annotated lane-marking frames and 9,494 driving segments. On held-out driving data across all four provinces, the student passes the consistency check in 94.6% of the segments, against 20.2% for the untuned 8B teacher, and announces 26.2% of the risks in the next 10 to 30 seconds before they appear. Of the generated advisories, 97.7% conform to the provincial traffic rules and guidance. These findings suggest that the framework can produce rule-conforming training data for compact driving advisory models.

### 17. [BehaviorICL: Task-Conditioned Decoder States for Visual In-Context Learning](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.28081)

**<font color=#1a73e8>作者：</font>** Chenyang Wang, Jiamo Hu, Chengrui Wu 等 7 位作者  
**<font color=#188038>arXiv所属领域：</font>** Computer Vision and Pattern Recognition

**<font color=#5f6368>摘要：</font>**
> The usefulness of an in-context demonstration for a vision-language model (VLM) depends on both the query image and the task posed about it. We study whether the frozen target VLM's own decoder states, read before answer generation, can select such demonstrations. BehaviorICL represents each image and task by a task-conditioned processing signature, the hidden states at the final input position across all decoder layers. Its training-free variant, BehaviorICL-Raw, averages cosine similarities between corresponding layer states and passes the retrieved examples with their reference answers to the same VLM. When the same NICO++ images are asked about their object or their context, Raw retrieves largely different demonstrations for the two questions and outperforms an image retriever given task-matched demonstrations on both VLMs. Across two VLMs and eight classification and visual question answering datasets, Raw gives the highest average accuracy among training-free retrievers on both VLMs and exceeds RICES with a CLIP ViT-L/14 encoder by 3.83 points on Qwen and 1.85 on InternVL. A lightweight shared projection, BehaviorICL-Proj, brings further gains on classification. Analyses of extraction text, decoder depth and bank answers help explain these gains, and attention analysis describes how the task text shifts attention at the extraction position toward the classified object. These results support the target VLM's internal states as a practical basis for visual in-context learning.

### 18. [VTree: Question-Driven Growth of Hierarchical Memory for Agentic Long-Video Understanding](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.28093)

**<font color=#1a73e8>作者：</font>** Xiaoyu Ma, Lianyu Hu, Zixuan Hu 等 7 位作者  
**<font color=#188038>arXiv所属领域：</font>** Computer Vision and Pattern Recognition

**<font color=#5f6368>摘要：</font>**
> Long videos spanning tens of minutes to hours far exceed the context window of today's vision-language models, and the standard remedy of sparse uniform frame sampling discards exactly the fine-grained evidence that specific questions depend on. Existing video agents explore on demand but re-explore from scratch for every question and discard their exploration state once an answer is produced, so per-question cost scales with video length. Hierarchical-memory methods instead fix the scope of each depth in advance and deepen along a pre-committed pyramid rather than toward the question. We observe that a video's coarse structure (where its scenes and topics begin and end) is question-independent, whereas which part a question must zoom into, and how deep, is question-dependent. We propose VTree, which builds a coarse hierarchical memory tree over a video once and then, for each question, grows branches only where that question needs depth. The growth is globally routed so any part of the timeline is reachable, sufficiency-gated so drilling stops when the evidence suffices, and query-conditioned so a branch unfolds into the sub-intervals that answer \emph{this} question. Crucially, every expansion is written back into the video memory, allowing later questions to reuse previously discovered structure and evidence without repeating the corresponding expansion process. Across five long-video understanding benchmarks, VTree consistently improves the accuracy of diverse vision-language backbones while substantially reducing the context length and execution time required for each question.

### 19. [LightOnOCR for AMNLT: Reading Chant Notation and Lyrics in One Pass, from Single Staves to Whole Pages](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.28094)

**<font color=#1a73e8>作者：</font>** Robert Klugseder  
**<font color=#188038>arXiv所属领域：</font>** Computer Vision and Pattern Recognition

**<font color=#5f6368>摘要：</font>**
> Aligned music notation and lyrics transcription (AMNLT) asks for the notes of a chant, its sung text and the assignment of syllables to notes in one output. Fuentes-Martínez et al., who defined the task, found that the one vision-language model they tested fell far behind specialised recognisers.
