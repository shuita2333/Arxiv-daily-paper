### 1. [Participant-Independent Validation of Smartphone Video and Wearable Inertial Sensing for Coach-Rated Running-Form Classification](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.27996)

**<font color=#1a73e8>作者：</font>** Kian Lee, Eric Zhou  
**<font color=#188038>arXiv所属领域：</font>** Human-Computer Interaction

**<font color=#5f6368>摘要：</font>**
> Automated running-form assessment could make technique feedback more accessible outside professional coaching and laboratory settings. We evaluated a low-cost pipeline combining side-view smartphone video with wearable inertial measurement unit (IMU) acceleration for coach-rated running-form classification. The dataset comprised 294 labeled recordings from 20 runners and 1,555 windows across four cues: landing position, suspension, triple extension, and upper-body posture. The primary analysis used 100 repeated four-runner holdout splits, recording-level aggregation of window predictions, and LightGBM models with hyperparameters fixed from prior modality-specific searches. Video-only LightGBM achieved mean macro F1 of 0.310, versus 0.168 for a majority-class baseline. Adding IMU features reduced macro F1 to 0.267, a paired mean difference of -0.043. Robustness analysis with Logistic Regression, Linear SVM, Random Forest, XGBoost, and LightGBM found that video-only models had higher mean performance than the majority baseline, whereas fusion effects depended on model family: Linear SVM showed a small positive effect, Logistic Regression was approximately neutral, and tree-based models showed lower performance with fusion. These results indicate a modest participant-independent signal in smartphone pose features. However, apparent IMU-fusion gains depended strongly on validation design, model family, and target category. The gap between recording-level and participant-independent performance underscores the need for runner-holdout evaluation and suggests that simple feature-level fusion does not reliably generalize to unseen runners.

### 2. [Environmental Interaction: Recognizing Capability within Everyday Actions](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.28002)

**<font color=#1a73e8>作者：</font>** M.C. Schraefel, Alexander Ng, Chris Tacca 等 9 位作者  
**<font color=#188038>arXiv所属领域：</font>** Human-Computer Interaction

**<font color=#5f6368>摘要：</font>**
> Systems designed to support everyday capability consistently ask people to do something additional: track activity, follow programmes. For those who need such support most, this ask is often too high. We propose a different approach: environmental interaction. Through four probe deployments with older adults we show that everyday actions are themselves generative sites for interaction design, and that engagement depends less on sensing and guidance than on revealing in that action that it is polymorphic. A chair sit-stand is also a box squat; a door pull also a row. Each time participants encountered this second frame, the response was surprise, delight, and "I can do that!" The paper contributes environmental interaction as a design space, a serial probe methodology, and empirically grounded design considerations - including invitation over instruction, recognition before extension, and interaction as presence - centered on recognition as a generative design challenge.

### 3. [Towards Objective Estimation of Physical Functioning Status in Breast-Cancer Patients using Real-World Data](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.28005)

**<font color=#1a73e8>作者：</font>** Alexandros Papadopoulos, Ioannis Sarafis, Vasileios Papapanagiotou 等 10 位作者  
**<font color=#188038>arXiv所属领域：</font>** Human-Computer Interaction

**<font color=#5f6368>摘要：</font>**
> Breast cancer is the most common invasive cancer in women, accounting for about one third of cancer cases. As with all cancers, its treatment may introduce side-effects like fatigue and reduced physical functioning, leading to a downward spiral of subsequent issues and severely affecting patient quality of life. On the positive side, physical functioning can be improved via personalized targeted interventions, tailored to the particularities of each case. However, continuous and objective monitoring of patients is a difficult task, owing to the limited clinical resources and the lack of appropriate tools. In this paper, we introduce a methodology for objectively estimating the physical functioning status of breast cancer patients from wearable smartwatch data. We show that features derived from longitudinal walking and heart rate measurements correlate significantly with phys- ical functioning scores of established patient-reported outcome measures. In doing so, our work takes a first step towards developing novel validated digital biomarkers for estimating physical functioning in breast cancer patients, thus reducing the need for self-reported measures and costly clinical evaluations.

### 4. [Measuring Human Resilience to Unreliable AI Assistance: A Stress-Test Framework for Human-AI Interaction](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.28006)

**<font color=#1a73e8>作者：</font>** Shaoshan Liu  
**<font color=#188038>arXiv所属领域：</font>** Human-Computer Interaction

**<font color=#5f6368>摘要：</font>**
> As AI increasingly mediates human decisions, users must remain effective not only when assistance works as expected but also when its quality deteriorates. Human--AI research has developed rich measures of trust, reliance, and calibration, while human--autonomy research has examined resilience at the team and system levels. A missing measurement level is the human decision maker: how much task function is preserved or recovered when AI advice becomes unreliable? We introduce the Resilience Stress Test, a challenge-conditioned HCI framework that measures performance relative to a prechallenge baseline and summarizes functional loss and recovery. Four construct-validation tests draw on five public human--AI studies comprising 1,074 analytic participants. A controlled decline in AI quality produced sharp degradation followed by partial adaptation; misleading advice reduced performance in art and dermatology; baseline adjustment substantially attenuated the apparent complexity gradient in trip planning; and a logical-reasoning tutorial improved self-assessment calibration while challenged performance remained stable. The framework makes human resilience to unreliable AI operational as a functional outcome distinct from competence, calibration, and reliance, and provides a prospective protocol for comparing interfaces under reproducible AI reliability stress.

The impact of how an agent presents itself and its knowledge becomes greater.

### 7. [Evaluating the Participation of Proactive Scouting Agents in Real-time Human Team Meetings](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.28009)

**<font color=#1a73e8>作者：</font>** Hans Schumann, Miles Bader, Louis Rosenberg 等 4 位作者  
**<font color=#188038>arXiv所属领域：</font>** Human-Computer Interaction

**<font color=#5f6368>摘要：</font>**
> An open research question is whether the addition of conversational AI agents to real-time discussions among human teams enhances group performance. This study tested the value of adding proactive scouting agents to teams engaged in collaborative forecasting tasks. These agents were designed to (i) monitor the groupwise discussion in real-time, (ii) identify critical information gaps in the group deliberation, (iii) autonomously find and retrieve relevant information, and (iv) express the acquired content into the conversation using emulated groupwise human etiquette. In a controlled set of trials, small human teams of 4 or 5 members were tasked with collaboratively predicting the outcome of 34 Major League Baseball (MLB) games, either by forecasting the winning team or by predicting the total number of runs scored. In half the trials, the groups were assisted by proactive scouting agents. The other half were human-only deliberations. When tasked with predicting the winning team, the addition of proactive scouting agents to the group discussion significantly increased forecasting accuracy, reducing the predictive mean-absolute error by 25% (p=0.006) and improving raw predictive accuracy from 60.0% to 76.4% (p=0.050). When predicting the total number of runs scored across both teams, the addition of proactive scouting agents yielded no statistical change in group performance. It should be noted that the human participants in this study were significantly worse at predicting the number of runs scored. This may have led to random outcomes in those forecasting trials. Still, the results of this study demonstrate that deploying proactive scouting agents can significantly boost forecasting accuracy during real-time team deliberations when participants possess baseline forecasting skill.

### 8. [Beyond Code and Pixels: Co-Generating Editable and Visually Rich Presentations](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.28010)

**<font color=#1a73e8>作者：</font>** Wenrui Zhou, Jiashun Liu, Sujie Hu 等 5 位作者  
**<font color=#188038>arXiv所属领域：</font>** Human-Computer Interaction

**<font color=#5f6368>摘要：</font>**
> Generating PPTX presentations is a structured task that requires semantic planning, visual composition, precise layout, and editability, motivating collaboration among specialized agents. However, programmatic methods offer editability but often produce rigid designs, while image-based methods provide visual richness but flatten content into difficult-to-edit pixels. A hybrid system can combine these strengths, but must coordinate interdependent decisions across content, layout, and appearance. Across 1,728 generations spanning 24 system-model configurations, SlidesWeaver achieves consistently superior overall quality across all evaluated dimensions while maintaining structural control, editability, and practical generation efficiency.

### 9. [Neurosymbolic Human-AI Collaboration: Foundations, Conceptual Framework, and Applications](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.28011)

**<font color=#1a73e8>作者：</font>** Vito Scaraggi, Andrea Passarella, Marco Conti  
**<font color=#188038>arXiv所属领域：</font>** Human-Computer Interaction

**<font color=#5f6368>摘要：</font>**
> The growing adoption of Artificial Intelligence (AI) across workplaces and industries, ranging from manufacturing plants to healthcare facilities, has paved the way to the design of systems in which humans and AI collaborate on shared tasks. The first developments of Human-AI Collaboration (HAIC) were driven by advances in Machine Learning (ML), which enabled the automation of numerous tasks, including large-scale data processing, image analysis, and speech recognition. However, ML automation have proven insufficient for certain applications due to methodological, safety, and ethical concerns. In such cases, researchers argue for integrating human expertise into operational workflows to improve performance, relying on the complementary skill sets of humans and AI. Recognizing the need for a shared interface between humans and AI in collaborative settings, we propose a novel conceptual framework, NeSy-HAIC, that leverages Neurosymbolic AI (NeSy AI) to bridge the semantic gap between machine representations and human-understandable symbolic concepts. The framework builds on current research in NeSy AI and HAIC, supports multiple interaction paradigms, including Human-In-The-Loop and Human-On-The-Loop, and is characterized through its enabling methodologies and two illustrative use cases in semi-autonomous driving and security alert prioritization. We argue that the integration of data-driven learning and symbolic reasoning can substantially enhance human-AI interaction and mitigate limitations of collaborative systems.

**<font color=#5f6368>摘要：</font>**
> The discussion surrounding artificial intelligence (AI) is primarily influenced by Western viewpoints, often neglecting the cultural, regional, and socio-political factors that affect global perceptions of AI. This study examines how these factors shape AI apprehension in various regions, including Africa, Asia, Latin America, and the Middle East. A qualitative literature review was conducted, analyzing peer-reviewed articles, policy reports, and gray literature from multiple regions. The study used a comparative approach to assess the cultural, political, and economic contexts shaping AI perceptions. ### 11. [When Modalities (Dis)Agree: How Decomposing Multi-Modal AI Advice Shapes Appropriate Reliance](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.28017)

**<font color=#1a73e8>作者：</font>** Nidhi Mishra, Joshua Holstein, Patrick Bigge 等 5 位作者  
**<font color=#188038>arXiv所属领域：</font>** Human-Computer Interaction

**<font color=#5f6368>摘要：</font>**
> Multi-modal AI systems integrate diverse data streams into unified advices, but this fusion conceals how individual modalities contribute to the final output. Consequently, decision-makers cannot observe whether underlying sources aligned or contradicted one another when assessing the provided advice. To test whether revealing this structure supports appropriate reliance, we conducted a pre-registered experiment (N = 240) in which participants evaluated used-car prices with multi-modal AI support. In a 2x2 between-subjects design, we varied the disclosure of modality-level outputs and natural-language explanations, crossed with within-subjects modality agreement. Revealing modality-level outputs increased reliance, while explanations improved decision accuracy independently. However, this occurred only when modalities agreed: visible consensus acted as a persuasive cue that generally amplified adherence, rather than helping participants override errors under conflict. Thus, decomposing multi-modal advice can make AI advice more persuasive without making errors easier to detect.

### 13. [SoundFleXR: Selectively Constraining Axes of Audio Rendering Presentations](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.28020)

**<font color=#1a73e8>作者：</font>** Brandon Woodard, Hyunju Kim, Hyunsung Cho 等 6 位作者  
**<font color=#188038>arXiv所属领域：</font>** Human-Computer Interaction

**<font color=#5f6368>摘要：</font>**
> Extended reality (XR) systems increasingly rely on audio cues to help users locate, interpret, and interact with virtual content, especially when the content is outside the field of view. To support this, many systems use head-related transfer functions (HRTFs) to approximate real-world acoustics. However, a realistic simulation is only one possible presentation. Audio can also be altered by constraining, flexing, or contracting the spatial dimensions represented in the signal. This work examines how selectively constraining or flexing the number of audio dimensions affects localization performance across XR task structures with different spatial degrees of freedom. We compare two-channel spatial audio, azimuth-only HRTF, and full HRTF rendering across orientation-based (3DoF) and translation-based (6DoF) tasks. Azimuth-only HRTF was mutually non-inferior to full HRTF for total angular error across task conditions, including at the strictest tested tolerance in 3DoF (5 degrees), and remained comparable for azimuth error at (10 degrees) and larger tolerances in both 3DoF and 6DoF. In contrast, two-channel stereo required coarser tolerances to reach non-inferiority, becoming comparable to full HRTF for 6DoF azimuth error at (15 degrees) but not (10 degrees). From these comparisons, we derive design lessons and application scenarios that indicate when fewer spatial cues provide sufficient support and when richer spatial cues are beneficial.

### 14. [REMORA: A Voice-First Memory Assistant for Grounded Personal Recall and Independent Living](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.28026)

**<font color=#1a73e8>作者：</font>** Bilal Jawaid, Muhammad Maaz Uddin, Osama Farooqi 等 4 位作者  
**<font color=#188038>arXiv所属领域：</font>** Human-Computer Interaction

**<font color=#5f6368>摘要：</font>**
> Most of what matters in a day is spoken and written down nowhere: a dosing interval named at a clinic, a deadline moved in passing, the place a document was set down. Such information is consequential because it is operational, and fragile because it arrives during divided attention -- the moment its importance becomes clear is rarely the moment it was said. For people living with mild cognitive impairment, Alzheimer's disease or age-related episodic memory decline, that gap bounds independent living. Conventional aids close it poorly: notes, reminders and calendars require the user to notice, interrupt and transcribe, adding extraneous cognitive load exactly where attention is already committed. We present REMORA, a voice-first memory assistant that converts consented speech into structured, auditable memory records. REMORA transcribes and diarizes captured audio, extracts memory candidates -- tasks, appointments, names, instructions, object locations -- each retaining provenance into the conversation it came from, and holds records, embeddings and relationships in a single relational system of record. Recall combines lexical, semantic, relational and recency signals, since no one signal covers the ways people interrogate their own past, and reaches the user either through an explicit query or through a confidence-gated cue that stays silent unless it is likely to help. Generation is bound by a grounding contract: only retrieved evidence may be used, and absent a supporting memory REMORA abstains rather than confabulates, because a fluent invention about one's own past will be acted upon as fact. A requirements survey and a pilot study with memory-loss users indicate that conversational forgetting extends well beyond clinical populations, that grounded and concise recall is feasible in controlled settings, and that privacy, rather than accuracy, is the binding constraint on adoption.

### 17. [Safety Systems Watch What the Model Says, Not What the Child Says](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.28029)

**<font color=#1a73e8>作者：</font>** Fatma Egal, Rehnuma Taskin  
**<font color=#188038>arXiv所属领域：</font>** Human-Computer Interaction

**<font color=#5f6368>摘要：</font>**
> Children are increasingly talking to consumer chatbots. We audit the published category definitions of 17 safety taxonomies and child-safety benchmarks. Combinations of these systems lead us to 144 categories whose definitions could be reliably coded: 104 classify properties of model output, 25 classify user intent, and 15 describe user-state or user-impact. Within these 15 user-state/user-impact categories, 10 attribute the relevant condition to the system, 1 to the user, 2 concern the user's disclosure of personal information, and 2 do not specify the responsible source. None of the coded categories identifies harm inflicted on the child by another person as the object of evaluation. Child abuse appears repeatedly across these taxonomies, but as prohibited content, prohibited generation, or prohibited user intent, not as an externally inflicted condition that a child may disclose to the model. Consequently, a child reporting abuse has no dedicated taxonomy label that identifies the disclosure as a safety-relevant event requiring a response distinct from content suppression. We ran five behavioural probes, grounded in the child-disclosure literature, through six publicly accessible consumer-facing chat models to examine the behavioural consequences of this omission. We propose no detection system. Instead, we prove that externally inflicted harm disclosed to an AI system remains unnamed in the evaluation vocabulary used by current safety taxonomies, and that naming the missing case is a precondition for measuring, comparing, and improving model behaviour toward such disclosures.

### 18. [WISP: Composable Representations for Wrist-Worn Activity Recognition](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.28033)

**<font color=#1a73e8>作者：</font>** Zipeng Wu, Shiqiao Zhou, Fabian Spill 等 4 位作者  
**<font color=#188038>arXiv所属领域：</font>** Machine Learning

**<font color=#5f6368>摘要：</font>**
> Smartwatches and fitness bands are among the most common wearable devices, and large national health studies record movement at the wrist. Yet existing human activity recognition benchmarks mix sensors worn on different parts of the body, so they do not show how well activities can be recognised from the wrist alone. We introduce WristHARBench, a benchmark that standardises participant-disjoint evaluation across nine wrist-acceleration datasets and two additional multichannel inertial datasets. We hypothesise that different human activities leave complementary patterns in wrist motion, such as local shape, rhythm, temporal order and cross-axis relationships, and propose WISP (Wrist-Informed Space of Predictive Representations), a composable representation space that combines representations of these patterns with compatible classifiers. Controlled ablations and baseline comparisons indicate that recognition improves mainly when a representation adds motion patterns that the others lack, rather than when it represents the same pattern in a different way.

### 19. [Following the Error Through a Compressed Network:An Angular Measure for FFN Weight Compression in ViT-L/16](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.28067)

**<font color=#1a73e8>作者：</font>** Paolo D'Alberto  
**<font color=#188038>arXiv所属领域：</font>** Computer Vision and Pattern Recognition

**<font color=#5f6368>摘要：</font>**
> We study the compression of the 48 feed-forward network (FFN) matrices of the Vision Transformer ViT-L/16 to 2--8 bits with four schemes: a per-token grid, a fixed Gaussian Lloyd--Max table, a table fitted to the matrix, and a random-sign Hadamard rotation followed by the fixed table. We evaluate on 50,000 images. At 4 bits the top-1 accuracy of the four schemes lies within 0.8 points of the float32 model, while the agreement with its predictions ranges from 98.4\% to 94.4\%. We use the angular error between the compressed and the float32 residual stream, followed through the blocks, as a portable measure that ranks the schemes as the final classification does and shows where the ranking is built: with rotation the angle grows close to the square root of the number of blocks, the signature of independently accumulating errors, while the other schemes are amplified in the later blocks. The same measure shows that a scheme that is better on one block in isolation can be worse in the whole network. To address this inconsistency we introduce an angular measure of the spectrum of the output of a block, which in isolation orders the schemes as the whole network does where they diverge most. We support these observations with real samples and with statistically generated ones. The scope is one model, the FFN weights only, and float evaluation of compressed weights; no speed-up is claimed. The error measures and the sample generation, however, can be applied to other models and precisions.

### 20. [Fairness Evaluation for Diffusion and Flow Models Should Account for the Generation Path: Implications for Clinical Imaging](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.28077)

**<font color=#1a73e8>作者：</font>** Dharini Raghavan, Amritpal Singh, Anant Madabhushi  
**<font color=#188038>arXiv所属领域：</font>** Computer Vision and Pattern Recognition

**<font color=#5f6368>摘要：</font>**
> Medical image generation models are often evaluated using images produced by a single sampling procedure. However, diffusion and flow models can use different numbers of sampling steps, sampling schedules, or numerical solvers without changing their trained parameters. These changes may affect the generated images and the clinical measurements derived from them. We argue that fairness results obtained under one sampling procedure should not be assumed to hold under another, particularly when changes in sampling affect clinical task errors differently across patient groups. Building on previous work on medical image quality, accelerated sampling, generation dynamics, and generative model fairness, we examine why the sampling procedure should be considered part of the evaluated system. We define subgroup differences in clinical task error across sampling procedures and provide a mathematical example showing that two groups can have identical outputs under the exact dynamics and a reference sampler but different errors under an alternative sampler. We also extend this result to stochastic generation under shared noise. An exploratory experiment with an age-conditioned image generation model illustrates how changing the number and timing of sampling steps can alter image-derived measurements, although these differences do not establish clinical unfairness. We therefore argue that fairness evaluation should assess the sampling procedure used in practice and examine intermediate generation states when they help explain differences in clinically relevant errors across patient groups.
