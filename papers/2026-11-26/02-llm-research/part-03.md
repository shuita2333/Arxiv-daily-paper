### 101. [Your VLM Knows What to Inspect: Semantic Probe-Guided Adaptive Focusing for GUI Grounding](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.31909)

**<font color=#1a73e8>作者：</font>** Ruixiao Shi, Fu Feng, Yucheng Xie 等 5 位作者  
**<font color=#188038>arXiv所属领域：</font>** Computer Vision and Pattern Recognition

**<font color=#5f6368>摘要：</font>**
> GUI grounding requires resolving fine visual details within the context that gives them meaning. Additional views can improve localization, but their value depends on what evidence they expose and how much context they preserve. We explore a capability of vision-language models (VLMs): even when their coordinate predictions are incorrect, they can describe visual evidence worth inspecting. We introduce \textbf{SPARK}, \textit{\textbf{S}emantic \textbf{P}robe-guided \textbf{A}daptive \textbf{R}egion see\textbf{K}ing}, a training-free framework that turns this semantic guidance into targeted visual observations. Exploration probes describe relevant context and distinguishing target details, while verification probes identify evidence needed to assess emerging hypotheses. Their spatial attention guides view placement, contextual responses determine crop extent, and observation history encourages complementary inspection. The resulting predictions are combined through uncertainty-weighted voting. On ScreenSpot-Pro, SPARK improves grounding accuracy over single-view inference by 13.9, 25.2, and 16.1 percentage points with Qwen3-VL, Qwen2.5-VL, and UI-TARS, outperforming all compared test-time methods for each backbone. Consistent gains across four additional benchmarks demonstrate its effectiveness across diverse interfaces and grounding tasks. These results establish semantic probing as an effective mechanism for turning a frozen VLM's understanding of what to inspect into more accurate GUI grounding.

### 102. [DUALingo: A Dual-Hand Instruction-Guided Dataset for Affordance Grounding on 3D Objects](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.31919)

**<font color=#1a73e8>作者：</font>** Ming-Lun Lee, Yi-En Li, Feng-Dian Su 等 7 位作者  
**<font color=#188038>arXiv所属领域：</font>** Computer Vision and Pattern Recognition

**<font color=#5f6368>摘要：</font>**
> Affordance grounding localizes where an agent should interact with an object to perform a task. Many everyday manipulation tasks, however, require bimanual affordance, in which two hands assume complementary and cooperative roles to accomplish a shared goal. Existing 3D affordance datasets primarily ground single interaction regions, and manually annotating dual-hand, cooperative contacts does not scale. To address this gap, we introduce DUALingo, a large-scale dataset for task-conditioned, role-aware bimanual 3D affordance grounding on diverse everyday objects. DUALingo represents each task as two cooperative, hand-agnostic roles, each grounded as a heatmap on the object surface. It contains 271K free-form tasks on 71K objects, generated by a fully automated pipeline of open-weight foundation models without human labeling. Expert ratings and a validated LLM judge confirm the quality of the resulting annotations. Trained on DUALingo, existing 3D affordance models learn to ground both cooperative roles from natural-language instructions, with the best-performing model achieving a more than threefold improvement in aIoU over the zero-shot baseline, from 10.3 to 37.8. Robotic manipulation demonstrations illustrate the actionability of the predicted bimanual affordances in performing cooperative tasks. These results position DUALingo as a scalable foundation for learning everyday bimanual affordances. Project page: this https URL

### 103. [Decompose, Verify, Evolve: Physically Grounded Shape Decomposition](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.31924)

**<font color=#1a73e8>作者：</font>** Wenchao Ma, Rui Yu, Fan Zhu 等 5 位作者  
**<font color=#188038>arXiv所属领域：</font>** Computer Vision and Pattern Recognition

**<font color=#5f6368>摘要：</font>**
> Shape decomposition represents 3D objects as a compact set of geometric primitives for interpretable modeling and simulation. However, existing decomposition methods are trained primarily for geometric fidelity, without explicit constraints on physical plausibility, and can therefore produce disconnected parts or physically unstable reconstructions. We present PhysDec, an agentic framework that draws on a pretrained vision-language model's semantic understanding and commonsense reasoning to predict material-aware primitive decompositions from a few images. Within this framework, PhysDec-Loop is an agentic pipeline that reasons about part structure and materials, using a reference 3D point cloud for geometric feedback and physical verifiers to guide iterative refinement. Through self-evolution, we further develop another agentic pipeline, PhysDec-Evolved, which learns from prior corrections to predict primitive decompositions from images without point-cloud feedback or a correction loop at inference. Experiments show that PhysDec-Loop improves geometric fidelity, physical plausibility and visual quality over geometry-focused baselines, while PhysDec-Evolved approaches its performance at substantially lower inference cost.

### 104. [Scoring What Matters: Exact Feedback Reduction for Language Model Comparison](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.31931)

**<font color=#1a73e8>作者：</font>** Hui Zhang, Haoda Yue, Bicheng Deng 等 6 位作者  
**<font color=#188038>arXiv所属领域：</font>** Computation and Language

**<font color=#5f6368>摘要：</font>**
> Comparing language-model checkpoints requires estimating performance differences, yet evaluation pipelines can pay to score responses whose contributions cancel from those differences. We introduce Contrast-Aware Feedback Acquisition (CAFA), a sampler-agnostic method that identifies which scores a requested linear report needs before acquiring feedback. CAFA groups identical scoring inputs and aggregates their report coefficients, removing queries beyond ordinary caching while preserving the fully scored result. For a fixed response bank and an otherwise unrestricted scalar scorer, we characterize necessary and sufficient query sets for exact recovery of sample means, either alone or jointly with ordinary sample covariances. On controlled updates, CAFA removes 55.8% and 90.1% of the scoring inputs remaining after caching for Qwen2.5-1.5B-Instruct and SmolLM2-1.7B-Instruct, respectively, averaged over four generation repetitions. Across three online group relative policy optimization (GRPO) trajectories, Independent Star sampling with CAFA achieves 46.1-51.3% lower recorded generation-plus-feedback cost than independent autoregressive sampling with caching at a common empirical precision threshold (RMS MCSE $\leq 0.04$). This is a post hoc comparison of completed runs on a fixed budget grid. All 504 evaluated report cells reproduce the fully scored means and covariances to numerical precision. We provide a modular implementation supporting existing response banks, versioned caches and external scorers.

### 105. [Contrastive On-Policy Self-Distillation for LLM Unlearning](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.31932)

**<font color=#1a73e8>作者：</font>** Fangzhou Lin, Shuo Xing, Xin Li 等 6 位作者  
**<font color=#188038>arXiv所属领域：</font>** Artificial Intelligence

**<font color=#5f6368>摘要：</font>**
> Large language model unlearning aims to remove the influence of designated training data while preserving unrelated model capabilities. Existing likelihood-based methods commonly optimize against fixed forget sequences, providing only negative supervision on what the model should not generate and overlooking the states visited by the evolving unlearned policy. We introduce Contrastive On-Policy Self-Distillation (CPSD), a new unlearning framework that constructs an explicit knowledge-reduced target distribution and distills it along the model's own trajectories. Given a student rollout, a frozen reference model evaluates each token under a plain context and a privileged context containing the forget information. CPSD treats the resulting distributional shift as a knowledge-associated direction and reverses it to obtain a counterfactual unlearning target. The student is then trained to match this target at every rollout position while retaining its utility on unrelated data. We further introduce a privacy-enhanced variant that combines distributional unlearning with lightweight sequence-level suppression. Experiments under OpenUnlearning show that CPSD improves the forgetting-utility trade-off over NPO across TOFU forget-set sizes and Llama models ranging from 1B to 8B parameters. On MUSE-News, the privacy-enhanced variant improves all reported knowledge, memorization, privacy, and utility metrics over NPO.

### 106. [CAPE: Coordinating Calibration and Search for Tool-Use Policies](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.31938)

**<font color=#1a73e8>作者：</font>** Jiahao Wang, Xiaoxuan Chen, Aijun Yang 等 4 位作者  
**<font color=#188038>arXiv所属领域：</font>** Artificial Intelligence

**<font color=#5f6368>摘要：</font>**
> A tool call can fail even when the operation behind it is correct, because the receiving tool parses delimiters, dates or record layouts under a configuration the agent cannot inspect. Compiling a tool-use policy for such a tool requires both calibration, which evaluates candidate calls on candidate configurations, and search over the evaluated calls. The two share one compilation budget: calibrating everything first postpones any improved policy until the end, while re-solving every partially known model delays the next useful query. A call becomes usable as soon as its effects are known on the configurations that reach it. We present CAPE, a training-free compiler built on this observation. It maintains an executable policy at all times, directs calibration to the calls that can improve this policy, and tightens bounds only where doing so ends calibration early. Returned policies preserve every configuration the initial policy completes, and CAPE certifies optimality when its executable and optimistic values meet. On CSV and JSON workflows built from 64 requests, CAPE reaches the independently computed optimum in 502 of 504 compilations while taking 22.0% less compilation time than eager exact search. On larger CSV families it returns policies with higher completion at similar compilation time, and it raises completion by 15.38 to 34.66 percentage points over an LLM that revises its serialization from error feedback.

### 107. [Beyond the Last Loop: Reinforcement Learning with a Teacher Extrapolated from the Model Itself](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.31939)

**<font color=#1a73e8>作者：</font>** Linghua Zhang  
**<font color=#188038>arXiv所属领域：</font>** Machine Learning

**<font color=#5f6368>摘要：</font>**
> Looped language models apply one shared transformer stack several times per token, and every loop can be read out through the shared language-model head. Post-training them with verifiable rewards is hard: the outcome reward carries one bit per response, spreading it over loops trains intermediate loops on tokens sampled from the last loop, and distillation needs either a teacher that ignores the reward or an external, larger model. We observe that a looped model already contains a teacher stronger than its last loop: the next-token distribution extrapolated from the first loop through the last. ELGO (Extrapolated-Loop Group-relative Optimization) adds to GRPO a dense per-token distillation term towards this distribution. The teacher is computed from an anchor copy of the model, which stays frozen and is replaced by the current model once the student's KL to the teacher falls below a threshold, so the teacher improves with the student without chasing its own extrapolation. The outcome reward splits probability between correct and incorrect responses, and the teacher decides which tokens are preferred inside a response. Between refreshes the objective is KL-regularised RL anchored at the teacher, whose optimum tilts the teacher by the reward. On Ouro-1.4B-Thinking, ELGO raises the average avg@8 over five math benchmarks from 62.32 with GRPO to 64.20 and is the best compared method on every benchmark, while spreading the reward over loops falls below the base model.

### 108. [Beyond "AI Can Make Mistakes": How Risk Communication Shapes Trust and Decision Confidence for LLMs in Health Information Seeking](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.31942)

**<font color=#1a73e8>作者：</font>** Jiawei Zhou, Mengting Tu, Shravika Mittal 等 6 位作者  
**<font color=#188038>arXiv所属领域：</font>** Human-Computer Interaction

**<font color=#5f6368>摘要：</font>**
> Adoption of LLMs for health information has outpaced public understanding, but whether risk communication can work as a corrective remains unknown. In a preregistered experiment (N=500), we compared a standard disclaimer against structured risk communication varying in contextual relevance and mechanistic explanation. Without intervention, users understanding LLMs less reported higher trust and confidence in deciding when to rely. Structured risk communication significantly reduced trust, more so with health-specific framing, but did not improve confidence in reliance decision. Risk disclosure was associated with higher decision confidence among those with stronger AI knowledge, but lower confidence among those less preferring analytical thinking. Effects were also task-dependent: disclosure shifted preferences for medical queries and impairment assistance, while LLMs remained preferred for emotional companionship regardless of exposure. These findings demonstrate that risk communication can narrow trust gaps across AI-literacy levels, but turning disclosure into judgment requires adapting to usage context and users' prior knowledge.

### 109. [VLG-Drive: Unifying Vision-Language and Geometric Priors for Autonomous Driving](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.31952)

We present VLG-Drive, which keeps both models frozen and trains only a fusion interface and a planner from trajectory labels. At each camera-grid cell, the interface fuses the paired VLM and geometry features into one joint token through a geometry-anchored residual, and a command-conditioned planner reads these tokens to predict the ego trajectory. These results indicate that a lightweight planner trained on fused features of frozen vision-language and geometric foundation models is an effective alternative to adapting either model for planning.

### 110. [Perception Is Enough, at the Right Scale: Scale-Adaptive Token Pruning for Vision-Language-Action Models](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.31954)

**<font color=#1a73e8>作者：</font>** Yifeng Lin, Liuxiang Qiu, Yunfeng Chen 等 4 位作者  
**<font color=#188038>arXiv所属领域：</font>** Artificial Intelligence

**<font color=#5f6368>摘要：</font>**
> Vision-language-action (VLA) models must process continuous streams of visual observations, and the resulting computational cost limits real-time robotic control. Token pruning reduces this cost, but a fixed semantic scale of perceptual representation can overlook information needed at different stages of manipulation. We show that perceptual semantics can guide effective token selection without relying on internal attention when the selection scale adapts to the scene's evolving semantic structure. Based on this observation, we introduce TauPrune-VLA, a training-free method that controls the scale of the retained perceptual representation through a single perception temperature $\tau$. To adapt this scale as manipulation proceeds, TauPrune-VLA updates $\tau$ online using trends in perceptual semantics, with robot-state changes and action magnitude regulating the adjustment strength. While these semantics evolve over the course of manipulation, they remain relatively stable between consecutive frames. This continuity allows retention indices computed from the current observation to be applied at the next policy invocation, enabling token selection to run asynchronously with inference and reducing pruning overhead. Evaluated across diverse VLA models and simulation benchmarks, TauPrune-VLA achieves up to a $1.86\times$ inference speedup while maintaining near-baseline task success rates. We further validate its effectiveness on two real-world robotic platforms.

### 111. [DexOPD: Dual-Extrapolation On-Policy Distillation for Diffusion Models](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.31969)

**<font color=#1a73e8>作者：</font>** Yunchao Ke, Yanzhe Hu, Yi Yang 等 6 位作者  
**<font color=#188038>arXiv所属领域：</font>** Artificial Intelligence

**<font color=#5f6368>摘要：</font>**
> Flow-based diffusion models have become a mainstream paradigm for text-to-image generation, where task-specific post-training produces specialist models with complementary strengths. Multi-teacher on-policy distillation (OPD) offers an efficient way to consolidate these specialists into a single unified student. Recent methods strengthen supervision through teacher extrapolation, constructing stronger proxy teachers that allow distilled students to surpass the original specialists. However, their extrapolation directions remain defined solely by frozen teacher and reference models, leaving the student's own training progress unexploited. To address this limitation, we introduce DexOPD, a dual-extrapolation on-policy distillation method for diffusion models. DexOPD retains extrapolated teacher supervision while leveraging a lagging historical student as the reference for student self-extrapolation. Sampling across multiple self-extrapolation gains from shared initial noise broadens exploration along the student's evolving trajectory, and task rewards convert pairwise differences in candidate quality into a normalized auxiliary regression direction. Empirically, we evaluate DexOPD on GenEval, OCR, and DrawBench. The resulting unified SD3.5-Medium student achieves a GenEval reward of 0.9736, an OCR score of 0.9515, and a DrawBench Aesthetic score of 6.4875, surpassing the corresponding specialist teachers as well as all evaluated OPD and multi-task reinforcement learning baselines across these three benchmarks while preserving standard single-model inference.

### 112. [Visual Thinking: Training-Free Active Perception for Counting and Localizing Hard-to-Find Objects](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.31973)

**<font color=#1a73e8>作者：</font>** Emil Kielar, Pratik Chaudhari  
**<font color=#188038>arXiv所属领域：</font>** Computer Vision and Pattern Recognition

**<font color=#5f6368>摘要：</font>**
> Vision foundation models can precisely segment objects based solely on a text prompt. However, they struggle to recover objects that are small, occluded or whose appearance is poorly captured by the original textual description. Vision language models can examine the image and its context, they can propose richer descriptions of the objects to be detected. But these models struggle with precise segmentation and localization. We introduce Visual Thinking, a training-free framework that combines a vision-language model with Segment Anything Model 3 (SAM 3) through an iterative active perception loop. At each iteration, the VLM proposes short noun phrases using the image, candidate crops and history from past iterations. A controller ranks these phrases using embedding similarity between the original prompt and the proposal, an entropy-based verification priority and the novelty of the prompt. Next, SAM 3 uses prompts to detect objects in the image. We associate detections across iterations and update each object's confidence score using a weighted mean of scores obtained from SAM 3 with a larger weight for detections using narrower prompts. We supply confident detections as positive visual exemplars for SAM 3, and incorporate image tiling to recover small objects in cluttered images. We evaluate counting and localization on CARPK, FSCD-147, PixMo-Count, and OmniCount (Fruits). Compared with SAM 3, Visual Thinking reduces the counting mean absolute error (MAE) by about 18 on FSC-147 and about 8 on CARPK. It improves box average precision (AP) by more than 12 on FSCD-147 and CARPK. Visual Thinking, with fixed or no tiling, performs on par with Claude Opus 5.5 at extra-high reasoning effort, while running on single desktop GPU in about 5.91 seconds per image, compared to about 46.53 seconds per image for Claude through its API.

### 114. [CodeHOI: Code with Agents for Physically Plausible 4D Hand-Object Interaction](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.31976)

**<font color=#1a73e8>作者：</font>** Seonghwan Park, Inho Chang, Min-Gyu Park  
**<font color=#188038>arXiv所属领域：</font>** Computer Vision and Pattern Recognition

**<font color=#5f6368>摘要：</font>**
> Modeling 4D hand-object interaction (HOI) is fundamental to understanding manipulation. This requires physics, since hands move objects only by touch and each material responds differently. Yet existing methods ignore this physics or simulate only a narrow range of matter. Programs can instead describe any matter with its physics, but coding agents omit the hand that drives the interaction. We close this gap with CodeHOI, to our knowledge the first agentic framework that writes physically plausible 4D HOI as programs. To write one, an Object Agent first builds each object with the physics of its matter. An HOI Agent then specifies the hands by their contacts and simulates each kind of matter with an established physics solver. Finally, an Evaluator Agent checks each result against physical laws. We also introduce CodeHOI-Bench with 100 reconstruction and generation tasks across four kinds of matter. With off-the-shelf frontier models, CodeHOI writes interactions that are plausible for every kind of matter and remain so when transferred to an independent physics engine. It thereby enables coding agents to write physically plausible HOI across diverse matter.

### 116. [Discovering Bandwidth-Efficient Prefetchers to Shrink the Cache Hierarchy](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.31983)

**<font color=#1a73e8>作者：</font>** Patrick Tser Jern Kon, Akanksha Jain, Abraham Gonzalez 等 14 位作者  
**<font color=#188038>arXiv所属领域：</font>** Artificial Intelligence

**<font color=#5f6368>摘要：</font>**
> Shared last-level caches (LLCs) increasingly dominate server processor die area, forcing architects to trade core counts for cache capacity. While intelligent data prefetching offers a pathway to reclaim this silicon, designing coordinated, multi-level prefetching policies for multi-core systems is bottlenecked by day-long, cycle-level simulations. This simulation wall limits even LLM-based microarchitecture discovery; the state-of-the-art ArchAgent system evolves one cache level at a time on single-core benchmarks, adds multi-core configurations only at the end, and trails the human champion under multi-core shared cache contention. We introduce DuoArchitect, an autonomous agentic framework for multi-fidelity, multi-core co-discovery of prefetchers. Agents jointly evolve evaluation proxies and coordinated prefetching policies across the L1D, L2, and LLC hierarchies, constructing simulation proxies 3-26x faster than cluster evaluation and hardware diagnostic oracles to guide the search. To study how much cache silicon a smarter prefetcher can replace, we evaluate and evolve prefetchers under reduced cache budgets, and find that evolving under our tightest cache constraint (with both the L2 and LLC halved) produced our strongest prefetcher overall at every cache size we tested: DuoArch-Nano. On the Fourth Data Prefetching Championship (DPC4) benchmarks with full-size caches, DuoArch-Nano delivers a 9.2% geomean IPC speedup (+10.5% on four-core mixes) while reducing four-core DRAM traffic by 11.4%, more than doubling the gains of the human champion BertiGO and the agentic ArchAgent v2. When the LLC is halved, DuoArch-Nano erases the baseline's 8.6% overall slowdown and edges out the full-LLC baseline overall (+0.88%), showing that agentic frameworks can generate prefetchers that reclaim megabytes of LLC SRAM per core with zero overall IPC penalty.

### 117. [InCue: Making Better Use of What Multimodal LLMs Already See at Test Time](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.31987)

### 119. [How Much Dependency Can a Marginal Rule See? Total Correlation in Diffusion LM Parallel Decoding](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.31990)

**<font color=#1a73e8>作者：</font>** Dovud Asadov, Rustambek Urokov, Qobiljon Toshnazarov 等 6 位作者  
**<font color=#188038>arXiv所属领域：</font>** Artificial Intelligence

**<font color=#5f6368>摘要：</font>**
> A masked diffusion language model is fast because it commits several tokens per step. Each token is sampled from its own marginal, so the dependency between the tokens is lost, and the size of that loss is the conditional total correlation C of the committed set. Most decoders choose the set from per-position marginals, which can bound C but cannot determine it. We ask how well they predict it.
