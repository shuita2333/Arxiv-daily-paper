### 151. [Physics-Guided Flow Matching: A Generative AI Framework for Global Total Electron Content Forecasting](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.37159)

**<font color=#1a73e8>作者：</font>** Si-Tong Yang, Feng Ye, Xian Lu 等 5 位作者  
**<font color=#188038>arXiv所属领域：</font>** Machine Learning

**<font color=#5f6368>摘要：</font>**
> Accurate forecasting of global total electron content (TEC) is important for space weather monitoring and the reliable operation of satellite communication and navigation systems. We propose a physics-guided generative artificial intelligence (AI) framework for day-ahead global TEC forecasting. The framework builds on flow matching, which learns to gradually transform samples from an initial distribution into new samples that share the statistical characteristics of the target data. The proposed model incorporates ionospheric dynamics described by a partial differential equation (PDE) into this transformation process, guiding the changes between initial and target samples using physical knowledge. The model further combines the physical dynamics with statistical patterns captured from data to learn this transformation, adaptively adjusting their relative contributions at each location. The model is trained and evaluated using one year of global TEC data generated by the Thermosphere Ionosphere Electrodynamics General Circulation Model (TIEGCM). The results demonstrate that our model improves both spatial and temporal forecasting accuracy over representative benchmarks, including statistical, deep learning, and generative AI methods at all lead times and across latitudes. The average root mean square error (RMSE) is 39.2% lower than that of ConvLSTM and 23.8% lower than that of the same framework without physical guidance. The learned contribution weights vary systematically with latitude and across geographic locations, showing how the physical contribution changes across the forecast domain. These findings demonstrate that explicit physical guidance improves generative forecasts of global TEC and highlight the value of incorporating physical dynamics into generative AI modeling.

### 152. [WorkFactory: Turning Professional Intents into Executable Work for Scalable Agent Training](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.37161)

**<font color=#1a73e8>作者：</font>** Ziyang Zeng, Tingyang Chen, Jia Deng 等 13 位作者  
**<font color=#188038>arXiv所属领域：</font>** Artificial Intelligence

**<font color=#5f6368>摘要：</font>**
> Scaling supervised training for working agents requires diverse professional tasks that yield reliable expert demonstrations. However, missing or inconsistent task information can obstruct teacher execution and weaken the resulting supervision. We propose WorkFactory, a scalable framework for constructing coherent and executable professional tasks. Starting from professional intents, information-level planning builds an information support graph to guide task instantiation, explicitly representing the dependencies and locations of information required for the intended outputs. Probe executions then identify support failures and guide repairs while preserving the intended work. We construct 112,686 tasks spanning 612 occupations and use the collected teacher trajectories to fine-tune Qwen models at 4B, 27B, and 35B-A3B scales, yielding the WorkPilot family. All three models improve over their respective base models across four professional-work benchmarks, with WorkPilot-35B-A3B improving from 879 to 1565 Elo on GDPval-AA v2.1. Scaling analyses reveal model-dependent diminishing returns, with execution length first increasing and then decreasing. Controlled component analyses show that planning and repair reduce probe-diagnosed task environment defects and improve downstream performance. The data and models are available at this https URL.

### 153. [LiveSearch: Questions as Executable Verifier Code for a Live Benchmark of Professional-Source Financial Search Agents](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.37166)

**<font color=#1a73e8>作者：</font>** Sinuo Wang, Wentao Zhao, Jiacheng Lu 等 9 位作者  
**<font color=#188038>arXiv所属领域：</font>** Artificial Intelligence

**<font color=#5f6368>摘要：</font>**
> In finance, the answer that matters is usually the current one: prices, rates, and disclosures hold only as of a given day, so retrieving up-to-date facts is a core capability of a financial agent. Yet most financial benchmarks overlook timeliness. They fix each answer at annotation time, so the gold goes stale as markets move and, once leaked into training data, rewards recall rather than search and analysis. We introduce LiveSearch, a live benchmark that stores each question as executable verifier code: a typed program of calls to a professional financial data service, followed by a declarative extractor. The question is frozen, while its gold answer is determined by executing the program against professional financial data at evaluation time, and a daily job re-executes it with no human or language model in the loop. A memorized answer is thus likely outdated, and a model must reach the current one through tools and analysis. Construction is equally automatic: a language model only verbalizes programs enumerated from real tool responses and never chooses an answer. Under general web search, six contemporary tool-calling models reach 70.9% to 76.7% answer accuracy. Over three consecutive days, 70% of served questions change their gold value, and even under tolerance-aware matching, a literally reused earlier snapshot fails on a majority of questions within a single day. Per-question tracking uncovers dynamic shifts in correctness masked by aggregate accuracy.

### 154. [MLE-World: Predicting Machine Learning Experiment Outcomes with a Learned World Model](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.37175)

**<font color=#1a73e8>作者：</font>** Brian Ko, Han Wang, Hejia Geng 等 5 位作者  
**<font color=#188038>arXiv所属领域：</font>** Machine Learning

**<font color=#5f6368>摘要：</font>**
> AI research agents can propose, execute, and improve programs that solve machine learning tasks. However, executing each candidate program requires training and evaluating ML models, limiting how many candidates an agent can explore at each step. We introduce MLE-World, a world model finetuned from Qwen3.5-4B that predicts a program's terminal output (error traces, training logs, and validation scores) from its code and data description without executing it, together with a confidence score estimating the prediction's correctness. Because a prediction costs one inference call rather than a training run, MLE-World makes test time scaling (TTS) affordable for these agents. We build five TTS policies, guided by MLE-World, that compare several candidates per step by their predicted outputs: Sequential revision, Best-of-N, Pairwise judging, Fusion, and a UCB style rule that also adds an exploration bonus from this confidence score. Since predictions can be wrong, we run a candidate for real only when its confidence score is low. On 17 MLE-bench Lite tasks, MLE-World matches the real terminal output on 77.9% of held out programs, versus 41.1% for much larger prompted models. MLE-World guided TTS achieves up to 22.1% higher observed mean performance than the agent without a world model, with less real execution. Project webpage: this https URL.

### 156. [Action-Value Calibration via Continuation Sharing for Long-Horizon Agentic Reinforcement Learning](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.37195)

**<font color=#1a73e8>作者：</font>** Yingjian Chen, Lang Zhou, Zixin Zhao 等 6 位作者  
**<font color=#188038>arXiv所属领域：</font>** Machine Learning

**<font color=#5f6368>摘要：</font>**
> Reinforcement learning for long-horizon agents relies on feedback from sequences of interactions, making credit assignment to individual actions difficult. Existing methods such as GiGPO compare actions at shared states to compute relative advantages, but estimate each action's value from a single sampled continuation. The resulting local credit remains sensitive to decisions made later in the trajectory. To address this problem, we propose ConCal (Continuation Calibration), a recursive continuation-value estimator that incorporates continuation evidence already present in a rollout batch. ConCal matches visits from the same task by their incoming state-action-state transitions, combines shared continuation evidence with each visit's own transition estimate, and propagates calibrated values backward to refine earlier action-value estimates. This reduces dependence on individual sampled continuations and mitigates local credit distortions without additional rollouts. Held-out return diagnostics show lower error in within-group value differences and higher agreement with reference action orderings than estimates based on individual continuations. With Qwen2.5-1.5B-Instruct, ConCal improves success rates over GiGPO by 3.39 and 5.86 percentage points on ALFWorld and WebShop, respectively. Our code is available at this https URL.

Evaluations show that 3D-SceneQ outperforms current state-of-the-art methods across multiple benchmarks (e.g., +5.7 CIDEr on Scan2Cap and +17.0 EM@1 on SQA3D), demonstrating its remarkable capabilities in various 3D language reasoning tasks.

**<font color=#5f6368>摘要：</font>**

### 159. [Uncovering Shortcut Amplification in Video RLVR through the Lens of Perceptual Uncertainty Collapse](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.37211)

**<font color=#1a73e8>作者：</font>** Jiafeng Liang, Xianhao Ou, Tao Ren 等 9 位作者  
**<font color=#188038>arXiv所属领域：</font>** Computer Vision and Pattern Recognition

**<font color=#5f6368>摘要：</font>**
> Reinforcement learning with verifiable rewards (RLVR) has emerged as an effective post-training paradigm for enhancing the video reasoning capability of Large Multimodal Models (LMMs). Despite its empirical effectiveness, we show that outcome-level optimization can introduce an undesirable shortcut amplification effect, whereby models increasingly favor text-prior answers even when they are not supported by the visual evidence. To characterize this behavior, we investigate the training dynamics of video RLVR through the lens of perceptual uncertainty, analyzing how predictive uncertainty evolves under video-conditioned and text-only contexts. Our analysis identifies a perceptual uncertainty collapse phenomenon, in which RL training continuously sharpens the model's answer distribution while the uncertainty reduction attributable to visual evidence progressively diminishes. A further gradient-direction analysis reveals that such confidence enhancement becomes increasingly less aligned with visually grounded uncertainty reduction and is instead dominated by text-prior compression. Motivated by these findings, we propose Bifocal Self-distillation Policy Optimization (BSPO), a self-distillation framework that regulates policy updates with two complementary distributional signals. Specifically, BSPO derives empirical reasoning principles from intra-group correct and incorrect rollouts to identify reasoning-sensitive tokens, and introduces shortcut-aware visual perturbation to penalize predictions that remain invariant when task-relevant visual evidence is weakened. By adaptively reweighting the original RL advantage, BSPO preserves the task-solving benefits of RLVR while promoting updates that are both reasoning-sensitive and visually grounded. Extensive experiments validate the effectiveness of BSPO in improving video reasoning performance and strengthening visual faithfulness.

### 161. [Vision-Language-Action or World-Action? Just Action Models](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.37219)

**<font color=#1a73e8>作者：</font>** Yonghao Yu, Lang Huang, Runyi Li 等 4 位作者  
**<font color=#188038>arXiv所属领域：</font>** Computer Vision and Pattern Recognition

**<font color=#5f6368>摘要：</font>**
> The vision-language-action (VLA) and world-action model (WAM) communities debate whether control policies should inherit pretrained knowledge from vision-language models or from visual generators. The debate, however, bundles three separable choices: backbone initialization, the objective applied to future observations, and whether the machinery for that future loss is kept at inference. A gap between a VLA and a WAM therefore cannot simply be attributed to any one of them. We show that decoupling these choices yields a common framework for both families, which we term Just Action Model (JAM): a pretrained visual backbone with an action expert, trained with an action loss and a foresight loss. The foresight loss trains current-observation features to predict frozen encodings of the recorded future through a prediction head discarded after training, so no visual generation is involved in either training or inference, and the backbone can be chosen freely. We first compare objectives within fixed policy architectures, then evaluate the foresight recipe across backbone families. The resulting JAM policies outperform their matched FastWAM and ImageWAM counterparts on LIBERO and LIBERO-Plus, and achieve similar or slightly higher success rates on RoboTwin. Compared with FastWAM, JAM improves the success rates from 97.0%/55.3%/91.2% to 98.2%/64.5%/91.4%, respectively. With cached foresight targets, JAM reduces estimated online training FLOPs per sample per update by 73% relative to FastWAM. Our results show that future supervision improves control without visual generation or additional inference machinery, and strong foresight performance is attainable with either generator-initialized or vision-language backbones. ### 162. [Decision Models as Judges for Hallucination Detection](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.37229)

**<font color=#5f6368>摘要：</font>**
> Large language models are widely used as judges of model outputs (LLM-as-a-Judge), but they are costly and can hallucinate or be overconfident. Decision models are a potential alternative. They return a predefined verdict with a probability for each label instead of free-form text and are much cheaper and faster. We test this promise on hallucination detection. We build binary benchmarks for five hallucination types (9,852 items from six datasets) and compare five decision models with the generative judge gpt-6-sol. We also find that a confidence cascade of decision models improves on the best single model by 1.9-3.3 points on three types and matches or exceeds gpt-6-sol at 1-3% of its cost. Overall, decision models are a practical low-cost judge for some hallucination types but need to be validated before use.

### 167. [Your Policy Is Secretly a Harness: The Decision Geometry of Agent Interfaces](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.37255)

**<font color=#1a73e8>作者：</font>** Jian Mu, Ruibo Shen, Yao Shu  
**<font color=#188038>arXiv所属领域：</font>** Machine Learning

**<font color=#5f6368>摘要：</font>**
> Language model agents are post-trained and deployed through a harness, the prompts, tools, and control logic that present a task to the model and execute its outputs. Harnesses are often revised and judged by how an already trained model performs under them, which assumes that what the model learned under one harness carries over to the next. We ask what a policy learns from its harness and what survives when the harness changes. Under KL-regularized control, we show that the log odds of an optimal policy recover how strongly its harness favors each action over the others, so a trained policy carries the preferences of its harness. Deployed return then equals the ceiling of the harness, the best return it offers, minus the mismatch of the policy, the return lost to its own suboptimal decisions. Training changes only the mismatch, while changing the harness moves both terms. Comparing harnesses with a fixed model therefore mixes what each harness offers with how well that model uses it. In MiniWoB++, post-training makes a model's preferences specific to its training harness. Reversing the order of the observation fields leaves every optimal decision unchanged yet lowers a trained model's success from 85.0% to 19.2%. Conversely, every new harness that a fixed-model comparison rejects but that raises the ceiling outperforms the original agent after further training under it, and ceiling and mismatch measured beforehand predict these gains. For coding-agent harnesses on Terminal-Bench Lite, ceiling minus mismatch estimated with a stronger model as a stand-in for the optimum reproduces the accuracy ranking, while either term alone does not. Code is available at this https URL.
